# Chemistry scope and review notes

The initial baseline is compulsory HKDSE chemistry, Topics V and XI. The implementation uses bounded straight-chain examples and syllabus-level reagent descriptions. It is not curriculum-certified or a universal prediction system.

Primary reference: [EDB Chemistry Notes for Teachers](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/science-edu/ref-and-resources/Supplementory_note_for_Chem_20180818_Eng.pdf), especially printed I-20–I-22, I-42–I-45 and reaction annex II-10–II-12. The annex was rechecked on 21 October 2026. The wider [EDB curriculum documents](https://www.edb.gov.hk/en/curriculum-development/kla/science-edu/curriculum-documents.html) and [HKEAA 2026 framework](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/DocLibrary/HKDSE/Subject_Information/chem/2026hkdse-e-chem.pdf) were used in the initial research. Recheck the intended examination-year requirements before classroom adoption.

## Implemented examples

- Addition: hydrogenation (Pt, Ni or Pd); Cl₂/Br₂/I₂ in organic solvent; HCl/HBr/HI with Markovnikov major-product selection.
- Substitution: methane monosubstitution; haloalkane hydrolysis; alcohol to haloalkane.
- Elimination: selected primary alcohols to terminal alkenes.
- Oxidation: primary alcohol to aldehyde or acid, secondary alcohol to ketone, aldehyde to acid.
- Reduction: aldehyde/ketone with NaBH₄(aq) or LiAlH₄ followed by work-up; acid with LiAlH₄ followed by work-up.
- Esterification and amide formation from an acid.
- Acidic and alkaline ester/amide hydrolysis, with optional subsequent acidification.
- Cracking into one alkane and one terminal alkene, accepting alternative atom-balanced pairs.
- Complete alkane combustion, requesting one CO₂ and one H₂O molecule, without coefficients.
- Addition polymers from ethene/propene; selected polyester/polyamide repeat units.

## Boundaries and grading conventions

Chains and ester/amide carbon totals are bounded at eight carbons. Secondary examples use position 2. Polymerisation uses a small set of monomers; initiator/heat/pressure wording is schematic. The editor and grader do not assess stereochemistry, mechanisms, balanced equations, reaction rates or yields. Methane's substitution steps are part of the curriculum but are not assessed by these modes.

Hydrolysis assesses only the carboxylic acid/carboxylate portion and omits the counter-ion. Ester hydrolysis displays the alcohol coproduct on the left; it is not part of the assessed drawing. Acid and carboxylate answers remain distinct. Cracking describes possible products, not a unique predicted mixture. Conditions must comprise one supported complete alternative; unrelated extra selections fail. “Then dilute acid work-up” always occurs last, regardless of selection order.

Polymer answers use exactly two `*` continuation points, one connected open-chain repeat, and single continuation bonds. Connectivity comparison permits shifted repeat boundaries by closing the repeat periodically. Multiple-repeat drawings, cyclic/backbone-crosslinked polymers and arbitrary polymer topologies are outside the current scope. Explicit hydrogen atoms and automatic hydrogens compare equally; unrelated extra molecular species are not ignored.

Some syllabus alternatives remain absent, including HF addition, PI₃ substitution, and the ordered PCl₃ then NH₃ amide procedure. Other oxidants/procedures, highly branched or multifunctional substrates, aromatic compounds and qualitative organic tests are not generated. A chemically valid procedure outside the supported selector may therefore require extending the rules before it can be entered or accepted.

The app's 2–4-step routes are shortest within its supported transformation graph, not necessarily among all real-world synthetic procedures. A teacher should audit conditions, explanations, accepted alternatives and scope before high-stakes use.

## Selector and synthesis wording (2026.4)

The official HKEAA [2023 Level 3 sample script](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/DocLibrary/HKDSE/Subject_Information/chem/2023-Sample-CHEM-Level3-E-3318.pdf), question 12, and [2022 Level 4 sample script](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/DocLibrary/HKDSE/Subject_Information/chem/2022-Sample-CHEM-Level4-E.pdf) provide synthesis-question wording: specify a route with a limited number of steps, the organic products, and reagents and conditions as appropriate. The 2023 question text was available through the search index; direct PDF retrieval returned 403. Candidate handwriting is not a marking scheme and was not used to establish accepted chemistry. The game’s 2–4-step range and XP are its own practice design.

- Pt/Ni/Pd share one hydrogenation catalyst choice. HCl/PCl₃ and HBr/PBr₃ share a choice only when the starting structure is an alcohol; phosphorus halides are not treated as alkene-addition reagents.
- State symbols replace “aqueous”. Organic solvent is included in halogen-solution choices; LiAlH₄ includes dry ether and the subsequent acid work-up.
- Alcohols, diol and diamine used as organic co-reactants are drawn in the left question panel. In synthesis, co-reactants are available for the route, but are used only when the learner checks “Use in step” in that specific step. These choices are graded alongside the other conditions. A co-reactant selected in an unrelated step is rejected. In single-reaction modes, the pictured co-reactant remains supplied automatically.
- A separate “excess oxidising agent” selection is removed. Acidified dichromate with reflux distinguishes full primary-alcohol oxidation from heating and distillation to an aldehyde. UV, catalyst, reflux, distillation and ordered acid work-up remain because they affect the transformation or isolated product.
- Heating and catalysts use compact selectors. No numerical temperature is required by these bounded rules; polymerisation conditions remain schematic.
- This change covers **esters** and condensation polymers. Ether synthesis and cleavage remain outside the implemented rules.

The final synthesis product is supplied as an assessment convenience. Earlier intermediates and every step’s conditions are still graded. The simplified selector is a teaching interface, not a claim that every listed reagent has identical laboratory handling or that its exact phrasing guarantees examination marks.
